# Consumer Protection Rules — Auto Sales in Delaware

## Delaware Consumer Fraud Act (6 Del. C. Chapter 25)

### Key Provisions for Auto Purchases
- Dealers must disclose all material facts about a vehicle's condition
- Deceptive trade practices include:
  - Advertising vehicles at prices the dealer does not intend to honor (bait-and-switch)
  - Failing to disclose prior damage, flood damage, or salvage title status
  - Misrepresenting the terms of financing or lease agreements
  - Adding undisclosed fees or charges after price agreement

### Buyer's Rights
- Delaware does NOT have a "cooling off" period or lemon law that allows returns of used vehicles
- New vehicles are covered under the Delaware Lemon Law (6 Del. C. Chapter 50)
- Right to a written purchase agreement before signing
- Right to review all financing terms before commitment

## FTC Used Car Rule
- Dealers must display a Buyers Guide on all used vehicles
- Guide must indicate whether the vehicle is sold "as is" or with a warranty
- Guide must list any included warranty terms

## Delaware Auto Dealer Licensing
- All dealers must be licensed by the Delaware Division of Professional Regulation
- License number must be displayed at dealership and on advertisements
- Complaints can be filed with the Delaware Attorney General's Consumer Protection Unit

## Best Practices for Buyers
- Always get trade-in value in writing before negotiating purchase price
- Verify VIN history through CARFAX or similar service
- Do not sign "spot delivery" agreements without understanding contingencies
- Request an "out-the-door" price that includes all taxes, fees, and charges
- Delaware sales tax on vehicles is 4.25% of the purchase price (Title 30, Chapter 53)

## Financing Disclosure Requirements (Truth in Lending Act)
- APR must be disclosed clearly
- Total cost of financing must be itemized
- Monthly payment amount and number of payments must be stated
- Any balloon payments must be clearly identified
